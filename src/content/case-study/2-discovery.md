## Approach

Discovery ran in weeks 1 and 2. The aim was to understand the work before changing it. I used three sources: the tickets themselves, the people who handle them, and the people who rely on them.

## Ticket audit

I exported two weeks of tickets from the shared inbox and read a sample from every day. For each ticket I recorded the channel, what the customer needed, the customer's tier, and how long the first reply took.

The audit answered three questions:

1. What do customers contact us about?
2. Which requests need a specialist?
3. Where does time get lost?

## Category taxonomy

The audit showed six kinds of request. I wrote one plain definition for each, so every agent sorts tickets the same way.

| Category | Definition | Who should own it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| License activation | The customer cannot activate, or the key is rejected | Technical |
| Download and install | The customer cannot get or install the product | Technical |
| Billing | Invoices, charges, renewals and payment problems | Billing |
| Refund | The customer wants money back | Billing |
| Account access | Login, two-step codes, account changes | Tier 1, with a security check |
| Other | Questions that fit no other category | Tier 1 |

## Agent interviews (simulated)

I held short one-to-one interviews with all 10 agents and both leads. The same themes came up again and again:

- "I spend the first hour of my shift sorting the inbox."
- "I pick up a refund, then find I cannot approve it, so I pass it on."
- "Enterprise customers phone the account manager because we are too slow."
- "I write the same activation steps many times a day."
- "Nobody tells me when a ticket is about to go stale."

## Findings

- **No routing.** Every agent sorts the same inbox, so work is done twice and specialist tickets wait.
- **No priority by customer.** Enterprise customers get no faster service than anyone else.
- **Hand-offs.** Tickets move between agents before they reach someone who can solve them.
- **Repeat writing.** The most common answers are typed from scratch.
- **No early warning.** Leads find late tickets only when a customer complains.
- **No measures.** There are no agreed targets and no weekly report.

These findings shaped every design choice in chapter 3.
